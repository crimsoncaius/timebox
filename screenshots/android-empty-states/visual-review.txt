ANDROID EMPTY-STATE VISUAL REVIEW — 29 OCTOBER 2026

Scope: independent sub-agent judgement of the 37 captured states, excluding Day.
This is a screenshot critique, not an interaction or accessibility certification.
The current calm, editorial style is the baseline. Empty space itself is not a
problem; disconnected hierarchy and unclear next steps are.

TOP PRIORITY: BATTLE PLAN'S EMPTY STATES

1. WEAK — Battle Plan tabs, Archive and Trash
   Evidence: 01-battle-plan-open.png, 02-battle-plan-in-progress.png,
   03-battle-plan-completed.png, 05-archive.png, 06-trash.png.
   The muted one-line message sits far below the filters/tabs. It reads like a
   leftover placeholder within a vacant canvas, rather than a designed state.
   In the board views the New task button is another detached visual island.
   Archive and Trash are even weaker: identical “Nothing here.” copy provides
   neither a specific state title nor an explanation of what belongs there.
   Direction: make a compact, consistently positioned title-and-explanation
   group within the content area. Relate the creation action to the Open state;
   explain In Progress/Completed instead of presenting creation as their answer.
   Archive/Trash need specific reassurance, not a forced primary action.

2. WEAK — Conversation before the first message
   Evidence: 15-conversation.png.
   Almost the whole screen is blank; the input's “What's on your mind?” is the
   only guidance. Nothing in the empty content area explains what this particular
   assistant helps with. The large New conversation control is visually prominent
   even though the current conversation is already new.
   Direction: one concise capability statement and two or three useful example
   prompts, placed near the composer. Preserve the quiet canvas; avoid a large
   illustration or generic welcome carousel.

3. WEAK — Habits and its empty Add habit sheet
   Evidence: 07-habits.png, 08-habits-no-candidates.png.
   The empty weekday scaffold dominates the actual state message. “No habits
   were active this week” is styled like a table footnote, followed by a strong
   Add habit button. In the no-candidate case that button opens a sheet containing
   only “Every active routine and checklist item is already a habit.” It looks
   like a dead end and is misleading for the captured fresh account with none.
   Direction: give the absence a clear heading; distinguish “no routines exist”
   from “all eligible routines are already tracked.” The former should visibly
   offer the prerequisite next step. Keep a historical empty week quieter than
   the first-use state. This is partly a guidance issue, not only styling.

4. WEAK — Task Type picker with an empty library
   Evidence: 36-task-type-picker-empty.png.
   The sheet shows “Unset” in the search field and again as its sole selectable
   row. It does not visibly explain that no Task Types exist or that entering a
   name can create one. The duplicate label makes the sheet feel unpopulated
   rather than intentionally empty.
   Direction: keep Unset as a legitimate choice, but give the field a real
   search/create hint and add a short, separate empty-library explanation.
   Preserve the concrete recovery row shown in 37-task-type-picker-no-match.png.

5. NEEDS POLISH — Time Goals
   Evidence: 12-time-goals.png.
   The content is useful, but the information icon floats alone between the week
   control and the text. Large intervening gaps split the date control, help,
   explanation and action into separate pieces. The neutral filled Add Time Goal
   button also reads quieter than equivalent blue add actions in this set.
   Direction: put help next to the relevant heading; tighten the title, supporting
   copy and action into one clear group. Review primary-action emphasis against
   the established button semantics before changing its colour. Preserve the
   explanation of how Actual Blocks supply progress.

6. WEAK COMPONENT — Google Calendar with no available calendars
   Evidence: 28-calendar-none.png; compare 29-calendar-unselected.png.
   Within the production settings component, account status and sync metadata
   lead; the actionable empty message is far below them. More seriously, “Select
   a calendar below” appears when there are no calendars to select. A standalone
   information icon and large gap further separate the explanation from recovery.
   Direction: collapse the empty imported-list section to a compact state beside
   its recovery action; show selection guidance only when choices exist. Keep
   “no calendars available” distinct from “calendars available, none selected.”
   These are fixture captures: the review title, scenario chooser and outer
   fixture spacing are NOT findings about the shipped screen.

7. NEEDS POLISH — Recurring Tasks, especially Paused and Ended
   Evidence: 09-recurring-active.png, 10-recurring-paused.png,
   11-recurring-ended.png.
   The active empty card is reasonably clear. On Paused/Ended, the bright New
   recurrence button remains the dominant element, although it does not explain
   the selected empty collection. “0 series” and “No ... series” repeat the same
   information, while the explanation about “lifecycle changes” is abstract.
   Direction: retain the compact card pattern, reduce redundant count emphasis,
   and explain the selected tab in concrete terms. Keep creation available as a
   secondary route on inactive tabs rather than the visual answer to that state.

STRONGER EXAMPLES TO PRESERVE

- 18-task-types.png and 19-task-types-no-match.png: compact title/explanation
  grouping next to the relevant input. The initial field hint could be clearer,
  but the state itself has a useful structure.
- 16-review-pending.png and 17-review-reviewed.png: restrained, specific copy
  explains what will appear. These do not need decorative artwork or a forced
  call to action simply because space remains below them.
- 37-task-type-picker-no-match.png and 26-project-no-match.png: recovery is local
  to the search context; the former provides a concrete creation action.
- 13-trends.png and 22-goal-no-recorded-time.png: retain the genuine zero and
  its range/period context. These are empty data within useful reporting views,
  so a large first-use illustration would add little.

OTHER OBSERVATIONS

The Assistant result-card fixtures (30–35) have understandable boundaries and
metadata hierarchy. Do not penalize their surrounding blank fixture shell as
production UI. 32-assistant-both.png has awkward “No both Blocks” wording, a small
copy issue rather than a weak visual composition. The empty shortcut preview
(21) is appropriately embedded beside the controls that populate it.

The shared improvement is a small family of empty-state treatments: first use,
empty collection, no search results, and zero data. Reuse spacing and hierarchy;
do not force identical copy, actions, icons or cards onto every case.
